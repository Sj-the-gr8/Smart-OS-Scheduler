What it learned from each workload

Stage 1 reads three signals from how FCFS, SJF, Priority and Round Robin did; Stage 2 tunes by simulation. A strong size signal pushes γ up (favour short jobs), a priority signal pushes α up, starvation pushes β up. The override threshold τ starts at the longest FCFS wait instead of the fixed 2 × average pages.

Where it is not smart yet: the convoy

In workload B a 50-page job arrives alone and starts printing, and five 1-page jobs arrive behind it. SmartQueue never interrupts a running job, so no choice of weights helps: SmartAdaptive waits — on average, while Round Robin, which interrupts every 3 pages, waits —. The next step is to let the learner also decide whether to interrupt, using how much Round Robin beats the others as its signal.

How the score works

Score = average wait weighted by each job's priority (1–5) + τ × share of jobs that waited longer than τ, where τ = 2 × the workload's average job size. The same τ judges every policy, so SmartAdaptive cannot look better by redefining starvation. Waits are in pages (1 time unit = 1 printed page). Workloads A–H are the project's test cases; KTH is 200 real jobs from the Parallel Workloads Archive (KTH SP2, 1996–97) mapped to print jobs. Weights are learned on the same workload they are scored on, like calibrating a spooler on its own print logs.
